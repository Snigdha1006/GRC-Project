Purpose of This Portfolio

This portfolio consolidates the four core artifacts of Vantage's information security program into one governed package: the governance documentation (Section 1), the enterprise risk register (Section 2), the Zero Trust architecture and incident response plan (Sections 3–4), and the board briefing (Section 5). A single-page executive summary precedes the body so that a first-time reader can understand the whole program without opening any individual section. Section 6 (Compliance Roadmap) and the appendices complete the package.

These artifacts were not authored in isolation. Every risk named in the board briefing is drawn from the register; every architecture decision traces to a register risk ID; and every capability the incident response plan assumes is operational today is funded and delivered by the roadmap timeline. Section 7 documents that traceability explicitly.

SECTION 1

Governance Documentation

The policy foundation that translates board intent into enforceable, auditable rules for the whole organization.

Governance is where a security program stops being a set of technical preferences and becomes an obligation the organization can be held to. The documents in this section are the program's constitution: they state what Vantage protects, who is accountable, and which behaviors are mandatory. They are written to be testable — every clause maps to a control an auditor can verify — and they deliberately use the same risk vocabulary (R-01…R-15) as the register so that policy, risk, and architecture never drift apart.

1.1 Information Security Policy (Master Policy)

Policy IDPOL-SEC-001OwnerChief Information Security Officer (CISO)
Approved byBoard Security & Technology CommitteeEffectiveOctober 2026
Review cycleAnnual, or upon material changeApplies toAll employees, contractors, and third parties with access

1.1.1 Purpose

This policy establishes Vantage's commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the information it holds on behalf of itself, its customers, and their end users. It defines the mandatory security objectives that all subordinate policies, standards, and procedures must satisfy.

1.1.2 Scope

This policy applies to all information systems, data, personnel, facilities, and third-party relationships that create, process, store, or transmit Vantage information — including production cloud infrastructure, corporate endpoints, source code, and any environment that handles customer or cardholder data.

1.1.3 Security Objectives

  1. Protect regulated data. Personally identifiable information (PII) and cardholder data must be protected at rest and in transit to a standard consistent with SOC 2, ISO 27001, and PCI DSS v4.0 expectations.
  2. Assume no implicit trust. Access decisions must be based on verified identity, device posture, and least privilege — never on network location alone (see Section 3).
  3. Detect and respond. The organization must be able to detect material security events and respond within defined severity-based timeframes (see Section 4).
  4. Manage risk explicitly. Security decisions must be traceable to the enterprise risk register (Section 2).
  5. Demonstrate compliance. The organization must maintain evidence sufficient to satisfy independent audit and customer due-diligence requests.

1.2 Access Control Policy

Policy IDPOL-SEC-002OwnerIAM Engineer (reporting to CISO)
Supports riskR-01, R-05, R-10, R-11Review cycleAnnual

1.2.1 Principles

Access is granted on the principles of least privilege, separation of duties, and zero standing privilege for administrative functions. No account may hold more access than the task it performs requires, and no access is granted on the basis of network location.

1.2.2 Mandatory Requirements

  1. Identity-first authentication. All access to Vantage systems authenticates through the central identity provider (IdP) with single sign-on. Local accounts are prohibited except for documented break-glass use.
  2. Phishing-resistant MFA. All workforce accounts and all administrative access to production require phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (FIDO2/WebAuthn). Legacy SMS and push-only MFA are being retired. R-01
  3. Just-in-time privileged access. Standing production administrator rights are prohibited. Elevated access is requested, approved, and time-boxed (maximum 8 hours), and every session is logged. R-05
  4. Quarterly access reviews. System owners certify that all access remains necessary. Orphaned and excessive entitlements are removed within 5 business days of identification.
  5. Joiner-mover-leaver automation. Provisioning and de-provisioning are driven by the HR system of record; termination revokes access within 1 hour.
  6. Service account hygiene. Non-human identities are inventoried, owned, rotated, and never share credentials with humans. R-10
  7. Device trust. Access to sensitive systems requires a managed, compliant device; unmanaged devices are denied. R-11

SECTION 2

Enterprise Risk Register

The single source of truth for the program — fifteen scored risks that give governance, architecture, response, and funding a shared vocabulary.

Every other section of this portfolio points back to this register. When the architecture removes implicit network trust, it is mitigating a specific risk ID. When the incident response plan assumes a forensic retainer is on standby, it is relying on a control that exists because a risk ID required it. When the board briefing asks for money, it names the risk IDs that money closes. The register is therefore not a compliance artifact filed away for auditors — it is the operating spine of the program.

2.1 Methodology

Risks are identified through threat modeling, incident post-mortems, audit findings, vendor assessments, and industry threat intelligence. Each risk is scored on a 5×5 likelihood × impact matrix, owned by a named accountable executive, and reviewed at least quarterly by the Security Steering Committee. Scores reflect inherent exposure given current controls; residual exposure after planned treatment is tracked separately.

SECTION 3

Zero Trust Architecture

How Vantage defends — removing implicit network trust and making every access decision identity-, device-, and context-aware.

Vantage's architecture is built on a single organizing idea: no user, device, or workload is trusted by virtue of where it sits on the network. Trust is never granted once and assumed thereafter; it is evaluated continuously for every request, using verified identity, device posture, and context. This is not a product we buy — it is a set of design decisions, each of which exists to reduce a specific risk in the register. The sections below describe each pillar and, critically, name the risk IDs it addresses.

3.1 Design Principles

  1. Verify explicitly. Every access request is authenticated and authorized using all available signals — identity, device health, location, and behavior.
  2. Use least privilege. Access is scoped to the minimum necessary and time-boxed; standing privilege is eliminated for administrative functions.
  3. Assume breach. The architecture assumes an adversary may already be inside; segmentation, encryption, and monitoring are designed to limit blast radius and shorten dwell time.
  4. Never trust the network. The corporate network is treated as hostile; the same controls apply whether a request originates from the office, home, or a public network.
  5. Make decisions traceable. Every control maps to a risk ID, so the architecture can be justified to auditors and the board.

3.2 Pillar 1 — Identity R-01 R-11

Identity is the new perimeter. All workforce and customer access flows through a central identity provider with single sign-on, and the strength of authentication scales with the sensitivity of the resource.

  • Phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn) is mandatory for workforce and administrative access; push-only and SMS factors are being retired. This directly attacks the credential-phishing vector behind R-01.
  • Conditional access evaluates device compliance, location, and risk score before granting a session; unmanaged devices are denied access to Restricted data, mitigating R-11.
  • Continuous evaluation re-assesses sessions on risk signals (impossible travel, token theft indicators) rather than trusting a session for its full lifetime.

3.3 Pillar 2 — Devices & Privileged Access R-05

Endpoints are inventoried, hardened, and continuously assessed for compliance. Administrative access to production is never standing.

  • Endpoint detection and response (EDR) is deployed on 95% of endpoints today, moving to 100%, providing telemetry that feeds detection (Section 4).
  • Just-in-time (JIT) privileged access replaces standing admin rights: elevation is requested, approved, time-boxed to a maximum of 8 hours, and fully session-recorded. This is the primary control against R-05 (privileged misuse).
  • Break-glass accounts are hardware-key protected, dual-approved, auto-expiring, and generate immediate alerts on use.

3.4 Pillar 3 — Application & API R-06

Vantage is an API-first platform, so application-layer access control is a first-class concern.

  • Central API gateway enforces authentication, rate limiting, and schema validation on every public endpoint.
  • Object-level authorization is tested automatically in CI/CD to catch broken access control before release — the root cause class behind R-06.
  • Secure SDLC integrates static analysis, dependency scanning, and IaC scanning into every pipeline; no build reaches production without passing security gates.

3.5 Pillar 4 — Network & Cloud R-04

The network is segmented and the cloud is continuously checked against a hardened baseline.

  • Microsegmentation separates production, corporate, and development environments; east-west traffic is denied by default.
  • Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA) replaces the legacy VPN, granting per-application access rather than broad network reach.
  • Cloud security posture management (CSPM) continuously detects misconfiguration (open storage, over-permissive IAM, exposed services) and drives automated remediation — the core control for R-04.
  • Policy-as-code guardrails prevent non-compliant infrastructure from being deployed in the first place.

3.6 Pillar 5 — Data R-07

Data protection follows the classification standard (Section 1.3) and assumes the perimeter will eventually be crossed.

  • Encryption of Restricted data at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+), with keys managed in a dedicated key service.
  • Tokenization of cardholder data so that production systems never store raw PANs.
  • Enterprise DLP and data-access governance detect and alert on anomalous exfiltration patterns — the detection layer for R-07.

3.7 Pillar 6 — Third-Party & Supply Chain R-02

  • Continuous vendor monitoring replaces point-in-time annual reviews for Tier-1 vendors.
  • Software bills of materials (SBOM) are ingested for critical dependencies, enabling rapid impact assessment when an upstream library is compromised.
  • Least-privilege integration ensures vendors receive scoped, revocable access rather than broad API keys.

3.8 Pillar 7 — Secrets & Developer Security R-10

  • Central secrets manager with automated rotation replaces hardcoded credentials.
  • Pre-commit and CI scanning block secrets from entering repositories; leaked keys trigger automatic revocation.
  • Non-human identity inventory ensures every service account has an owner and a rotation policy.

3.9 Pillar 8 — Vulnerability & Attack-Surface Management R-14

  • Continuous vulnerability scanning across cloud workloads, containers, and endpoints, replacing monthly point scans.
  • Risk-based patch SLAs: critical (internet-facing) within 15 days, high within 30, medium within 90.
  • External attack-surface management continuously discovers exposed assets and unknown internet-facing services.

Incident Response Plan

How Vantage responds — a NIST SP 800-61-aligned plan that assumes only the capabilities the program actually operates today.

An incident response plan is only credible if it reflects reality. A plan that assumes a 24×7 in-house SOC, a fully automated SOAR platform, or a forensic team on payroll would be fiction — and fiction collapses the first time it meets a real incident. This plan is deliberately grounded: it describes exactly the detection and response capabilities Vantage operates in October 2026, and it flags the capabilities still being built so that no assumption is silently unfunded. Section 6 confirms that every "operational today" claim below is already paid for, and that every future capability is on the roadmap.

4.1 Purpose & Scope

This plan defines how Vantage detects, contains, eradicates, and recovers from security incidents, and how it communicates during them. It applies to all production systems, corporate systems, and third parties with access to Vantage data. It is aligned to NIST SP 800-61 (Computer Security Incident Handling Guide) and supports the detection/response objective of the master policy (Section 1.1) and risk R-12.

4.4 Incident Response Lifecycle (NIST SP 800-61)

PhaseKey activities
1. PreparationMaintain playbooks, run quarterly tabletop exercises, keep the forensic retainer current, ensure EDR/SIEM coverage, and train the on-call rotation.
2. Detection & AnalysisMDR and SIEM generate alerts; the on-call analyst validates, scopes, and assigns severity. Indicators are enriched with threat intelligence before escalation.
3. ContainmentIsolate affected hosts, revoke credentials/tokens, and block malicious infrastructure. Short-term containment first, then a longer-term strategy to preserve evidence.
4. EradicationRemove adversary presence, patch the exploited vulnerability, and eliminate persistence mechanisms. The DFIR retainer is engaged for SEV-1/SEV-2.
5. RecoveryRestore systems from known-good backups, validate integrity, and monitor closely for re-infection before returning to normal operations.
6. Post-Incident ActivityConduct a blameless post-mortem within 10 business days, capture lessons learned, update playbooks and the risk register, and report metrics to the Steering Committee.
